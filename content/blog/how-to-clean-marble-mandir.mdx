A marble mandir becomes part of everyday family life. Lamps, flowers, incense and offerings all need space, and a simple cleaning routine helps you care for the stone without making daily pooja complicated.

Start with the care instructions supplied for your actual mandir. Inlay, painted details, metal fittings and storage components may need different treatment from the surrounding marble.

## Set up the platform for easy care

Use removable trays for lamps and offerings so that they can be cleaned away from the mandir. Choose trays that sit steadily and lift them when cleaning instead of sliding them over the surface.

Keep cleaning cloths in a convenient place and allow room to reach behind the idols without catching carved details. If your mandir has drawers, separate pooja storage from cleaning products and damp cloths.

When planning a new piece, discuss these daily habits with the maker. The [marble home temple design guide](/blog/marble-home-temple-design-guide) explains how storage, platform depth and decorative details affect access.

## Use a gentle cleaning method

Remove loose dust with a soft cloth. For ordinary cleaning, use a neutral stone cleaner or mild detergent as directed, rinse away residue and dry with a soft cloth. Avoid excessive water around fittings and joints.

Do not use lemon, vinegar or acidic bathroom cleaners on marble. Abrasive pads can also harm the finish. These basic principles follow the [Natural Stone Institute's stone-care guidance](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/).

For delicate carved or inlaid areas, ask the supplier which tools and products are suitable. Keep the product label so that anyone helping with cleaning can follow the same instructions.

## Deal with pooja spills promptly

Blot oil or ghee rather than spreading it across the platform. Lift away loose powders carefully, then follow the supplied cleaning method. Test an unfamiliar compatible product in an inconspicuous area before wider use.

If an oil, kumkum or haldi mark persists, photograph it and ask a stone-care specialist for advice. Identify the material, finish, any known treatment and the substances already used. Avoid trying a succession of household pastes, solvents or stronger cleaners on the same patch.

## Distinguish a stain from surface damage

A coloured mark and a dull patch may need different treatment. Acid can etch marble, so a dull area may not be removable through cleaning. Scrubbing harder can make assessment and repair more difficult.

Ask for professional assessment if the polish changes, a chip appears or a crack develops. Keep the area accessible and take clear photographs in ordinary light so the adviser can understand the problem.

## Ask before applying a sealer

Discuss whether a sealer is appropriate for the specific marble, finish and use. A sealer can improve stain resistance, but it does not make marble stain-proof or protect it from acidic cleaners. Follow the product and supplier instructions rather than using a universal resealing timetable.

Record the product name and application date if a treatment is used. This information is useful when requesting future maintenance advice.

## Make care part of the handover

When receiving a new mandir, ask for cleaning instructions covering the platform, carving, inlay, storage and fittings. Confirm whom to contact if the surface is accidentally damaged, and keep the sample and order specification for reference.
