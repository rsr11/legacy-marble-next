Choosing marble for a temple or fountain starts with the actual stone, not just its name. A catalogue label or a small photograph cannot show the full colour variation, veining, finish or treatment of the material that will be used for your order.

For a white marble temple or white marble water fountain, ask the supplier to connect the quotation, approved sample and final piece through a written specification. This guide explains what to check.

## Treat trade names as a starting point

Buyers may encounter names such as Makrana White, Vietnam White, Rajnagar White, Makrana Kumari, Makrana Pink, Albeta and Semi Albeta. Ask the supplier what each name means in the quotation and what source documentation is available.

These labels alone do not establish a particular grade or guarantee that every block has the same appearance. Ask to see the proposed material and confirm availability for the dimensions and carving you want. Do not assume a manufacturer carries every variety mentioned in an online guide.

When discussing a Makrana white marble temple, for example, ask for the specific material sample and the information supporting its description. Apply the same approach to a Vietnam white marble mandir or Rajnagar white marble fountain.

## Look beyond the whitest photograph

Review the sample under ordinary daylight and under the lighting planned for the room or garden. Ask for unfiltered photographs that include a neutral reference and close views of the surface. For a fountain, ask whether a wet sample can be shown as well.

Decide how much natural variation you are comfortable with. A large temple assembled from several pieces may not look identical to a small sample at every point. Ask how the maker will select adjoining sections and whether you can review their appearance before assembly.

## Ask about treatments, repairs and finish

Request a description of any resin, filler, reinforcement, coating or other treatment used on the proposed stone. Ask which treatments may be visible and what care instructions apply to them.

Approve a finished sample if possible. A raw offcut does not demonstrate the final sheen or how a carved edge will look. Record whether the sample represents the material colour, the finish, or both, so the approval is clear.

## Match the discussion to the product

### For a marble home temple

Review the appearance of pillars, the back panel and the main platform together. Intricate carving and jali should be assessed for the proposed dimensions and support arrangement. Ask how visible joints, corners and storage interfaces will be detailed.

Tell the maker about daily pooja use, including lamps and offerings. Use the [home temple planning guide](/blog/marble-home-temple-design-guide) to develop the layout before selecting every decorative feature.

### For a marble water fountain

Ask the supplier to explain why the proposed material and finish suit the location and water-feature design. Discuss the local water conditions, exposure, cleaning access and any maintenance treatments.

Check how the bowls, pedestal and basin will be matched visually. Our [fountain buying guide](/blog/marble-water-fountain-buying-guide) covers the equipment and site questions that should accompany the stone selection.

## Keep a material approval record

A useful approval record includes:

- The supplier's material description and available source information.
- Dated sample photographs and a sample reference.
- The accepted range of colour and veining.
- The agreed finish and disclosed treatments.
- The sections or surfaces covered by the sample.
- A process for agreeing any substitution before production.

Keep this record with the quotation and drawings. If a supplier proposes a different stone later, review the sample and specification again before accepting it.
