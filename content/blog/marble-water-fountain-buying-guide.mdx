A marble water fountain should suit the way you use a space, as well as the way it looks. A courtyard centrepiece, a quiet indoor feature and a fountain at a hotel entrance have different requirements for splash, sound, access and maintenance. Choose the location and water system before you finalise the carving.

This guide helps you prepare a practical brief for a custom fountain. You can use it alongside the [Legacy Marble fountain collection](/products/fountain) when discussing a design with our team in Makrana, Rajasthan.

## Start with the location

For a marble garden fountain, photograph the setting from the places where people will see it: the entrance, seating area and windows. Mark nearby paths, planting and seating on a simple sketch. Leave room around the basin for cleaning rather than filling the whole available area with stone.

An indoor marble fountain needs a different conversation. Ask how splash will be contained, how the floor will be protected and where the pump can be reached. Listen to a demonstration of the proposed water flow if possible. A sound that feels pleasant in a courtyard can be distracting beside a desk or television.

For a hotel, resort or farmhouse, share how guests move through the area. The fountain should leave a clear route and allow routine maintenance without blocking an entrance.

## Compare fountain designs

### Two, three and four tier fountains

A tiered marble fountain creates several falling-water levels and a strong vertical shape. A 3 tier marble fountain is worth considering for an open courtyard, but tier count alone does not tell you whether it will fit. Ask for the total height, widest bowl diameter, base footprint and receiving-basin size.

More tiers change the lifting, assembly and water-flow requirements. Review the whole composition in an elevation drawing rather than choosing from a cropped photograph.

### Bowl, lotus and cascade fountains

A marble bowl fountain can give a lower, broader silhouette. Lotus-inspired carving adds detail to a bowl or pedestal. Cascade designs place more attention on the route the water follows. Ask the maker to show where water leaves each surface and where it returns to the reservoir.

### Wall and waterfall designs

A marble wall fountain can suit a boundary or courtyard wall when a freestanding centrepiece would interrupt movement. Check the supporting structure, waterproofing, pipe route and service access early. A decorative marble panel does not replace a designed water-retaining system.

### Sculptural and ball fountains

Peacock, lion, horse and ball fountain references can help communicate a preferred style. Send a reference and ask whether the proposed form can be made, how it will operate and which components need servicing. Availability should be confirmed for the individual design.

## Choose the marble from a sample

For a white marble water fountain, ask to see the proposed stone in ordinary light and, where possible, when wet. Colour, veining and surface repairs can be less obvious in a small edited photograph.

Request written confirmation of the material name, source information available from the supplier, finish and any treatment. Our [marble selection guide](/blog/white-marble-for-temples-and-fountains) explains how to compare samples without relying on a trade name alone.

## Plan the equipment before carving

Ask for a drawing that identifies the pump, reservoir, overflow arrangement, drain and service opening. Confirm who selects the pump and checks its suitability for the planned water height and flow. If you want a marble fountain with lights, discuss cable routes and maintenance access before production.

The stonework, plumbing, electrical work and foundation may be supplied by different people. Have the responsibilities written into the quotation. See the [fountain installation checklist](/blog/marble-fountain-installation-and-care) before preparing the site.

## Compare complete quotations

Two similar photographs can represent very different supplies. One quotation may cover only the carved stone; another may include the basin, equipment, packing or assembly. Compare dimensions, material, scope and delivery terms line by line.
