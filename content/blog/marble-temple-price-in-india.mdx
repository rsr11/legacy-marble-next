A marble temple price is meaningful only when you know the design and what the quotation includes. Two mandirs described as the same size can differ in usable platform area, stone thickness, carving, storage and assembly requirements.

Instead of choosing from an unexplained price range, prepare one specification and use it for each quotation. This guide helps you compare a marble mandir price in India on a consistent basis.

## Define the complete dimensions

Share the overall width, depth and height, along with the desired platform height and usable idol area. Ask whether the dimensions in the quotation include the dome, projecting mouldings and base.

A broad frame with heavy pillars may leave less usable space than a simpler mandir with the same external width. Review an elevation and a plan drawing before treating two quotations as equivalent.

For a small flat or living room, the [home temple design guide](/blog/marble-home-temple-design-guide) explains how to balance the platform, structure and room around it.

## Record the marble being quoted

“White marble mandir” is a description of appearance, not a complete material specification. Ask for the trade name, source information the supplier can provide, finish, thicknesses and an approved sample.

Clarify the acceptable variation in colour and veining between parts. Ask whether the material has visible repairs or treatments and how these will be documented. Avoid comparing a sample-approved quotation with one that leaves the stone unspecified.

## Separate carving from additional features

The labour in a plain architectural frame differs from a deeply carved temple with detailed pillars, jali and a dome. Ask the maker to identify which decorative elements the price covers.

Discuss CNC work and hand finishing in terms of the intended pattern, depth, edge detail and final appearance. If inlay is included, specify the design and materials. A reference image should be accompanied by notes on which details will be reproduced or adapted.

## Specify storage, doors and the back panel

Drawers and doors introduce materials and hardware beyond the carved marble. Record the internal dimensions and the material of the storage carcass, shutter or door. Clarify whether handles, hinges, lighting and wiring are included.

For a back panel, confirm its size, support, visible seams and any lighting provision. Changes to these parts after production can affect both cost and installation.

## Ask what happens after production

Packing, delivery and installation should be separate, visible lines in the quotation. Give the supplier the delivery postcode and access details, including the floor, lift and staircase dimensions.

Ask who handles unloading and movement inside the property. Confirm who assembles the mandir, prepares the support, completes any electrical work and makes good surrounding finishes. Delivery to the building and a fully installed temple are different scopes of work.

## Compare the final specification

Before committing, collect these details in writing:

1. Approved overall dimensions and usable platform dimensions.
2. The material sample, finish and stonework specification.
3. Carving, jali, inlay and dome details included in the price.
4. Storage, doors, back panel and hardware scope.
5. Packing, freight, taxes, unloading and installation charges.
6. Approval stages, payment terms and estimated production schedule.
7. Inspection, transit-damage reporting and aftercare arrangements.

If the design exceeds your budget, ask about specific revisions. Reducing decorative complexity or reworking storage may preserve the layout you need. Compare the revised specification before accepting a lower figure.
