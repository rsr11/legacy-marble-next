A marble home temple works best when its proportions suit the room and its layout supports daily pooja. Before choosing a dome, carved pillars or a decorative back panel, decide what needs to fit inside the mandir and how your family will use it.

The right brief helps a maker develop a design that is both practical and personal. Here is how to plan a marble mandir for home, from the first measurements to the final drawing.

## Measure the space and the access route

Record the available width, depth and height, including skirting, sockets and nearby doors. Photograph the wall or alcove straight on. Mark any obstructions on a simple sketch rather than relying only on room dimensions.

Measure the idols and pooja items you want to keep on the platform. Allow comfortable room for their arrangement and cleaning. Consider whether pooja will be performed seated or standing, and discuss a suitable platform height with the people who will use it.

For a flat, also check the lift, staircase and doorway route. A marble temple may need to be designed in sections for delivery and assembly. Have the installer confirm the support requirements for the proposed weight and location.

## Choose a modern or traditional direction

### Modern marble mandir design

A simpler composition can suit a living room or compact apartment: restrained mouldings, fewer visual layers and a clear central platform. A back panel can give the mandir definition without requiring a large projection into the room.

Ask for an elevation drawing that shows the temple against the surrounding wall. The drawing should include visible joints and the relationship between the platform, frame and any lighting.

### Traditional marble temple design

Pillars, arches, carved borders and a dome can create a more architectural form. Review how much of the available width is occupied by the pillars and how much usable platform remains. More ornament should not make everyday access awkward.

A marble temple with jali work can add patterned detail, but ask how the openings will be cleaned and supported. If the design includes inlay, approve its colours and pattern separately from the carving.

## Plan storage and doors deliberately

For a marble mandir with storage, list the items first: pooja books, cloths, spare lamps and small containers may need different compartments. Agree drawer dimensions, hardware and the material of the internal storage structure.

If you prefer a marble temple with doors, check the opening clearance and whether the design needs ventilation. Ask the maker to explain the door material, hinges and support. A photograph of the closed front does not show how comfortably it will work each day.

## Discuss lighting and daily use

Decide where lighting will be installed and how its wiring can be serviced. Keep the cable route in the approved drawing rather than adding it after the stonework is finished. Let the qualified installer select suitable electrical components.

Consider how lamps, incense and offerings will be placed and cleaned up. Removable trays can make daily care more manageable. Read our [marble mandir care guide](/blog/how-to-clean-marble-mandir) when choosing details around the main platform.

## Select the actual marble sample

If you want a white marble temple, ask to approve the proposed stone rather than only a colour description. Review veining, tone, surface treatment and the finished sample in ordinary room light.

Our [white marble guide](/blog/white-marble-for-temples-and-fountains) covers the questions to ask about Makrana White, Vietnam White, Rajnagar White and other trade names. Availability and suitability should be confirmed for the individual design.

## Approve a complete design before production

Keep the dimensions, material, carving references, storage details and installation scope together in one written specification. Ask what changes are possible before approval and which choices become difficult to alter once carving begins.
