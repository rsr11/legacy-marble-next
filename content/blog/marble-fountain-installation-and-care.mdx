Marble fountain installation begins before the stone reaches the site. The foundation, basin, pump and service access need to work together. An attractive fountain becomes difficult to maintain if the pump cannot be reached or the surrounding area cannot drain.

Use this checklist to coordinate the stone supplier and site team. Structural, plumbing and electrical specifications should be agreed by the qualified professionals responsible for your project.

## Confirm the complete design first

Ask for a drawing showing the overall height, bowl diameters, base and receiving basin. Mark the location of joints, pipes and service openings. For a large marble water fountain, request the assembly sequence and approximate component weights so the installer can plan access and lifting.

Decide who is responsible for the stone, foundation, waterproofing, pump, plumbing and electrical work. Include those responsibilities in the quotation. This is particularly useful when a landscape contractor prepares the site and a separate supplier provides the carved fountain.

If you are still choosing a design, start with our [marble water fountain buying guide](/blog/marble-water-fountain-buying-guide).

## Check the location and access route

Measure the route from the unloading point to the final position, including gates, doorways, turns and overhead restrictions. Check that equipment can reach the site without damaging paving or planting.

Leave working space around the feature. Garden growth should not cover a service opening or make it difficult to clean the basin. For indoor installations, agree protection for the floor and finishes during transport and assembly.

## Have the base and basin designed for the project

A fountain foundation must suit the actual load and ground or floor conditions. Ask the site professional to confirm the support arrangement before dispatch. Do not select a foundation thickness from a photograph or a general blog recommendation.

The receiving basin needs a coordinated plan for water retention, return flow, overflow and draining. Confirm which surfaces retain water and who tests the waterproofing before the fountain is assembled. Arrange the surrounding finish so routine splash does not create a persistent slip hazard.

## Make the pump accessible

Have the pump supplier check the required flow and head against the final design. Identify a service opening that permits removal and replacement of the equipment. Record the pump model and keep the operating instructions.

For a marble fountain with lights, involve a qualified electrician in selecting and installing equipment suitable for its location. Keep electrical controls accessible for maintenance and follow the equipment maker's instructions before any cleaning or servicing.

## Test before signing off

At handover, run the assembled feature and check the following together:

1. Water follows the intended route through each level.
2. The basin contains ordinary splash at the agreed operating setting.
3. Joints, connections and the basin show no apparent leaks during the agreed test.
4. The pump and service openings remain reachable.
5. Overflow and drainage arrangements are explained.
6. Equipment documents and care instructions are handed over.

Photograph the completed assembly and hidden service routes before landscaping closes around them. Agree who to contact if a problem develops and which maintenance tasks fall to the owner.

## Set up a manageable care routine

Inspect the water level, clear debris and follow the pump manufacturer's maintenance schedule. Local water conditions and exposure affect cleaning frequency, so start with regular checks and adjust the routine to what you observe.

For the marble, use a compatible neutral stone cleaner and soft cloth, then rinse as directed. Avoid acidic descalers: marble is sensitive to acids. Persistent deposits or changes in the finish deserve advice from a stone-care specialist rather than a stronger household cleaner. The [Natural Stone Institute care guide](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/) explains the basic cleaning principles.
